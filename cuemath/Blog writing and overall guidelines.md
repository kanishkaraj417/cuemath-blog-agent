## Part 1: Blog Standard Structure and Flow

Every blog written or received must have these things, in this order:

1. Blog Title
2. Meta Description
3. Blog Excerpt
4. Introduction
5. Table of Contents — structured as per the decided theme
6. Content
7. Internal linking of similar blogs, blogs that will help readers, and CTAs
   - Internal Cuemath blog links (cuemath.com/blog/...) need **no Rebrandly link and no UTM parameters**.
   - Any CTA or link going to the Cuemath website (signup, app, pricing, etc.) **does** need a Rebrandly short link with UTM parameters.
8. FAQs

## Part 2: Writing Style & Execution Guidelines

The primary reason readers abandon online articles is readability friction. Avoid overly formal academic language, robotic AI tropes, and indirect sentence structures.

### 1. Ban Robotic AI Phrasing & Clichés

Certain words and introductory phrases immediately signal generic, low-effort content.

**Banned words:** delve, tapestry, paramount, testaments, unravel, crucial, realm, game-changer, seamless, elevate, foster, demystify, holistic.

**Banned setup phrases:**
- "In today's fast-paced digital world..."
- "It is important to remember that..."
- "Here is a comprehensive breakdown of..."
- "When it comes to [topic], many people struggle with..."

**The Golden Rule:** If you wouldn't say a sentence out loud to a colleague or client over coffee, do not write it.

### 2. Eliminating Passive Voice (The Action-First Rule)

Passive voice makes writing feel weak, academic, and distant. Always put the actor (the person or thing doing the action) before the verb.

- Passive (avoid): "A 3-minute diagnostic test should be taken by students before starting Algebra 2."
- Active (use): "Students should take a quick 3-minute diagnostic check before starting Algebra 2."
- Passive (avoid): "When New Math strategies are used by parents, homework battles can be avoided."
- Active (use): "Parents can end homework battles by using simple 'New Math' visual strategies."

### 3. Clear, Conversational Language Over Complex Vocabulary

Never use a multi-syllable, complex word when a short, everyday word conveys the same meaning faster.

| Complex / Pretentious Word | Clear / Human Alternative |
|---|---|
| Utilize | Use |
| Facilitate | Help / Lead |
| Elucidate | Explain / Show |
| Commence | Start |
| Paramount | Essential / Top priority |
| Endeavor | Try / Effort |

## Part 3: Audience Empathy & Strategic Framing

High-performing content matches the reader's exact emotional state at the moment of their search query.

### 1. The Reader Emotion Archetypes

**The Curiosity/Casual Reader** — seeking entertainment, quick brain boosters, or fun logic puzzles.
- Tone: energetic, sharp, lighthearted.
- Core offer: quick wins, shareable facts, 5-minute interactive games.

**The Anxious/High-Stakes Reader** — worried about grades, high school course placement, or AP/college credit.
- Tone: reassuring, authoritative, highly structured, clear.
- Core offer: diagnostic checks, clear roadmaps, GPA protection, gap-closing tools.

**The Frustrated/Confused Reader** — struggling with unfamiliar curriculum standards ("New Math") or homework battles.
- Tone: empathetic, supportive, simplifying.
- Core offer: visual method translators, side-by-side cheat sheets, zero-stress explanations.

### 2. The "1-in-1,000" Title Strategy

Out of 1,000 competing Google search results, generic titles get ignored. A title must establish immediate stakes, high novelty, or a direct answer to an urgent dilemma.

**Formula:** [Specific Hook / Emotional Stake] + [Actionable Payoff]

- Generic (avoid): "Back-to-School Math Activities for Middle School"
- High-converting (use): "10 Zero-Prep Middle School Math Warm-Ups That Beat First-Week Awkwardness"
- Generic (avoid): "Is AP Precalculus Worth It?"
- High-converting (use): "AP Precalculus: Does It Give College Credit or Just Trap Your GPA?"

## Part 4: Structure & Formatting Standards

### 1. The Rule of 2 Sentences (No Intro Fluff)

Never spend the first 300 words explaining why a topic matters. The reader already knows why they searched for it — they want solutions immediately.

- Sentence 1: validate the reader's core frustration or intent directly.
- Sentence 2: state the exact promise or payoff of the article.
- Sentence 3+: jump straight into bullet points, tables, or actionable advice.

### 2. Scannability Formatting

- **Markdown tables:** convert any itemized, multi-variable comparison (e.g., AP vs. Honors, Area Model vs. Standard Algorithm) into markdown tables.
- **Bullet points:** use liberally for sequential steps, key specs, or resource lists to break up dense blocks of text.
- **Standalone bold text:** use bolding for distinct section groupings instead of excessive formal headings for quick lists.

## Part 5: In-Content CTA Strategy (Dismantling Friction)

Depending on the blog theme and content, decide whether the CTA should send readers to a website page, a free evaluation (`/parent/signup`), or the Cuemath app.

### The Reassurance Triad — include at least two in every CTA block

1. **100% Free:** explicitly confirm no payment is required.
2. **No Credit Card Needed:** eliminates the fear of auto-renewing subscription traps.
3. **Low-Time Commitment:** frame product usage in micro-habits ("3-minute diagnostic check," "5-minute daily puzzle").

### CTA Placement Matrix & Templates

**Placement 1 — Top of blog (post-intro hook)**
- Context: high curiosity or acute initial planning stress.
- Focus: low-friction lead magnet (cheat sheets, quick diagnostic, free resource library).
- Template: `[Bold Benefit Headline • 100% Free • No Credit Card]` + 1-2 sentences tying directly to the hook above, offering an instant, zero-risk tool to solve the exact problem just read about.

**Placement 2 — Mid-content (post-core comparison or strategy section)**
- Context: reader recognizes their own or their child's blind spots/gaps based on the blog's content.
- Focus: diagnostic skill checks and prerequisite gap identification.
- Template: `[Find Hidden Learning/Test Blind Spots (Without Paying a Dime)]` + acknowledge the difficulty or complexity of the topic just discussed, then offer a quick 3-minute interactive check to verify understanding without pressure.

**Placement 3 — Bottom banner (post-conclusion)**
- Context: reader has finished, deciding whether to close the tab or take action.
- Focus: comprehensive feature summary and zero-risk invitation.

## Part 6: Pre-Publish Checklist

Run through this before submitting any piece:

- [ ] **Active voice check:** are all sentences structured with the actor performing the verb?
- [ ] **AI word audit:** are words like delve, tapestry, paramount, and seamless completely removed?
- [ ] **Title test:** does the title stand out against 1,000 competing Google results with clear stakes or payoff?
- [ ] **Zero-fluff opening:** does sentence 1-2 jump directly into actionable value or structured content?
- [ ] No keyword stuffing
- [ ] No vague qualifying language — replace "generally," "typically," "tends to" with specific data
- [ ] Every claim or factual statement leads with the verifiable source — no build-up leads
- [ ] Content matched to the right format:
  - Cost/pricing → table or FAQ
  - How-to/process → numbered steps, not paragraphs
  - Comparison → comparison table, not running text
  - No paragraph over 5 sentences
- [ ] Every paragraph makes sense as a standalone 2–4 sentence chunk without surrounding context
- [ ] 6–8 FAQs minimum (more is fine if genuinely specific)
- [ ] Every FAQ answer: first sentence is the direct answer
- [ ] FAQ questions phrased conversationally — how a parent would type into ChatGPT or Perplexity, not formal headings
- [ ] FAQ JSON-LD in Ghost code injection (Post Header), not post body
- [ ] Keyword placement: title tag, H1, first paragraph, 2+ H2s, FAQ questions, image alt text
- [ ] Image filenames descriptive: `cuemath-grade5-math-tutor.jpg`, not `image1.jpg`
- [ ] Alt text: accurate and contextual, not keyword-stuffed
- [ ] FAQ schema + Article schema added in Ghost code injection

## Part 7: AEO Optimization & Ghost Editing Mechanics

*(Restored 2026-08-13 from the archived style-guide.md, where it lived as a 2026-07-16 update from the "Is Cuemath Good for High School" build.)*

### Headings must match search/ask intent
- Every section H2 = the exact question a parent types into Google or asks an AI (ChatGPT/Perplexity), OR the direct answer to it. No brand-speak headings.
  - Wrong: "Cuemath Helps Accelerate Systematically: Hear From Our Students"
  - Right: "Can Cuemath Help a High Schooler Get Ahead and Reach AP Calculus?"
- Front-load the target keyword. Ground phrasing in real queries (check Ahrefs; keywords such as "high school math tutor" and "conceptual understanding in math" show a `question` SERP feature + AI Overview, so question-form headings get pulled into PAA / AI Overviews).

### Answer-first sections (extractable for AI Overviews)
- Every content section must open with a direct, self-contained answer, then go deeper. Pattern: "Yes, Cuemath helps [X] by [Y], because [Z]," then details/stories/cards.
- Never open a section with a lead-in ("Let's look at the data first"), a stat card, or a story card. If the section leads into cards/stories, put a 1–2 sentence answer paragraph before the card ("Here is what that looked like for real students:").
- Same for FAQ answers: first sentence is the answer, program named explicitly.

### Meta description = answer-first
- Lead with the answer, not the question: "Yes, Cuemath is good for high school...", not "Is Cuemath good for high school?"
- Name concrete proof (student success stories, real numbers). Avoid vague verbs ("I followed," "I looked at") — say what the reader gets. 150–160 characters, keyword + value promise.
- The SEO title may stay in question form; answer-first is not required in the title.

### Flow and structure
- Intro opens with the direct answer plus the reviewed-by note.
- Cut brand-speak sections and redundant "how it works in practice" wrap-ups; keep it tight.
- Fit-check section = a crisp "best for / not for" table, one concluding line, and one CTA. Not long bullet lists.

### Presentation
- Break dense sub-sections into scannable icon cards: icon tile + short bold H3 + 2 tight lines. Avoid walls of text.
- Card titles stay real `<h3>` with anchor ids (for SEO), even inside kg-card HTML.
- One toned-down card color per set (neutral card background + single accent icon tile), not rainbow — matches Ghost's light theme.
- Buttons: one consistent style/color across a section, no mixed primary and outline.
- "Best for vs. not for" comparison table: multi-row check column vs. a single X column; the imbalance is the message.
- All custom HTML uses inline styles only (Ghost posts have no stylesheet), wrapped in a kg-card, mobile-friendly (flex-wrap, shrinking icon). Text inside cards is fully crawlable — safe for SEO/AEO.
- Reusable card markup lives in `cuemath/html-templates.md`.

### Ghost editing mechanics
*(Only applies when editing a post that's already published in Ghost — `/blog` never needs this for new posts, since `ghost_uploader.py` sends plain HTML and Ghost converts it automatically.)*
- Edit lexical surgically at node level (match nodes by their text, not a fixed index); always PUT with a fresh `updated_at` (optimistic locking), or Ghost will reject the write as stale.
- When headings change, or sections are added or removed, rebuild the Table of Contents to match the live sections, and re-verify every ToC anchor resolves to a real heading id. Ghost slugifies heading text: lowercase, strip punctuation (`: , ? ' "`), spaces become hyphens, "1:1" becomes "11".
- Preview visual or HTML changes in an Artifact before pushing to Ghost.
